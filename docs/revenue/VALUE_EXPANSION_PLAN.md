# CityAtlas Value Expansion Plan

Last updated: 2026-07-01

## Current Readiness Verdict

CityAtlas is honest for a tiny request-first paid-traffic test into reviewed business requests. It is not yet fully self-serve charge-ready because one real City Partner checkout is still missing.

## Highest-Leverage User Value

CityAtlas should feel less like a thin directory and more like a confident local planning layer. The clearest user value is helping someone choose the right Vancouver plan quickly, then giving them enough structure to act without over-planning.

Priority additions:

1. Mood-and-situation route bundles: date night, rainy day, first visit, hosting guests, low-effort Sunday, birthday dinner, solo reset, family afternoon, visitor weekend, first-date, late dessert, scenic walk, no-car day, and quick downtown evening.
2. Route comparison pages: "Which route should I open?" pages that compare 3-5 plans by time, weather, transit friction, energy level, cost level, and best fit.
3. Save/share route cards: a clean shareable plan card with route name, why it fits, first stop, second stop, backup, and official source links.
4. Plan B fallbacks: every public route should have a rainy, low-energy, and sold-out fallback.
5. Time-boxed itineraries: 90-minute, two-hour, half-day, and full-day versions of the strongest routes.
6. Neighborhood chooser: a compact Vancouver chooser for Gastown, Mount Pleasant, Kitsilano, False Creek, UBC, Commercial Drive, West End, and Downtown.
7. Source-backed confidence cues: visible "reviewed from official sources" notes, correction links, and last-reviewed dates where practical.
8. Better route imagery: route-level mood visuals for situations, place-level visuals for individual anchors, and venue photos only when the specific business is being discussed.

## Highest-Leverage Business Value

Businesses will pay when CityAtlas can show qualified local intent, placement context, and a simple path from discovery to request.

Priority additions:

1. Route placement previews: each business gets a private preview showing how it would appear inside a route, not just as a listing.
2. Monthly visibility report: views, clicks, saves, route appearances, request starts, and correction/confidence status.
3. Business claim/correction flow: let owners request edits, add booking links, submit offers, and confirm facts before public promotion.
4. Founding Partner proof packet: a short owner-facing PDF/page with live route screenshots, placement example, audience, pricing, and what is still manual.
5. Offer submission lane: restaurants, cafes, wellness, culture, and experience businesses can submit one reviewable offer for route inclusion.
6. Sponsored route anchors: limited paid placements for one route category at a time, with clear review boundaries.
7. Category sponsorship tests: Date Night, Rainy Day, Visitor Weekend, Wellness Reset, and Family Afternoon are the strongest first categories.
8. Follow-up automation after requests: request received, review status, approved/needs edits, and checkout handoff only after review.

## Best Next Route Batch

Add depth before adding breadth everywhere. The best next batch is 8-10 high-intent Vancouver routes with real source-backed anchors, matching route-level imagery, guide-page pairings, and business monetization angles:

1. Date Night: dinner plus second stop, first-date variant, low-friction transit variant.
2. Visitor Weekend: first evening, one scenic morning, one culture/rain fallback.
3. Hosting Guests: impressive but easy local route, family-friendly variant, dinner finish.
4. Rainy Day: cafe, gallery/library, indoor market, and evening fallback.
5. Low-Effort Sunday: brunch, gentle culture, short walk, no-reservation backup.
6. Birthday Dinner: higher-intent restaurant route, dessert/drinks second stop, booking-link emphasis.
7. Wellness Reset: sauna/spa/studio, quiet food, park or waterfront reset.
8. Scenic No-Car Day: transit-light route with waterfront, market, and short walk.
9. Family Afternoon: weather-safe, washroom-friendly, food nearby, minimal transit drag.
10. Solo Reset: coffee, bookstore/gallery/library, walk, low-pressure meal.

## CRO Priorities

1. Make the homepage prove depth above the fold: more obvious route categories, better images, and a clear "choose by situation" path.
2. Make each route page feel actionable: first stop, second stop, backup, who it is best for, time needed, friction level, and source links.
3. Make pricing feel connected to proof: "get reviewed for a route placement" before asking for checkout.
4. Keep the paid test request-first: ads should drive to a business request/review flow, not a hard self-serve payment claim.
5. Keep one real checkout as the separate charge-readiness proof gate.

## Stop Conditions

- Do not claim fully self-serve charging until a real City Partner checkout succeeds.
- Do not imply generated route visuals are literal photos.
- Do not widen paid spend until request quality, page behavior, and checkout handoff are rechecked on the hosted domain.
- Do not publish business claims or rankings that are not source-backed or owner-reviewed.
